# Voice: why your brain does that

Psychology explainers that name an experience the viewer thought was only theirs, then give the mechanism behind it.

## Format

- Narrated cards over calm stock footage. One fixed text-to-speech voice, `en-US-AriaNeural`. One narration line per on-screen card. Music sits under the voice.
- 20 to 45 seconds.
- No face on screen, ever. The voice is the identity.

## Identity anchors

1. The voice. One voice for every clip. Never rotate it.
2. The grade. Neutral, calm interiors and nature, low contrast.
3. The structure. A hook card names a felt experience. Two to five beat cards give the mechanism. A landing card gives the reframe. Every card is spoken.

## How it sounds

- Second person, present tense: "your brain", "you do this because".
- Recognition, then relief. Name the private experience precisely, then give the mechanism that explains it.
- Short spoken sentences. If a line cannot be said in one breath, it is two cards.
- Warm and specific. Never mystical. Never clinical.

## Registers

- mechanism: what your brain is doing when something happens.
- naming: there is a name for this experience, and here it is.
- reframe: the behaviour is not what you assumed it was.
- tiny-experiment: something to try the next time it happens.
- applied: a sentence to say or a move to make the next day.

In the applied register the last card is the deliverable. It is a line the viewer could say out loud tomorrow, or one concrete change to what they do. It is offered, never ordered: "you can say", "the thing that works is". It is about what the viewer says or does, never about what they have.

## Hook

The first card and the first spoken line decide the clip. In the first two seconds, name a private experience the viewer thought was theirs alone.

## Footage

Window light, slow nature, quiet rooms, rain, steam from a cup. No faces.

## Guardrails

- No face, ever. One fixed voice.
- No link, handle or call to action. The account links to nothing.
- Not therapy. No diagnosis, no trauma claims, no medication talk, no "signs you have" content. Mechanisms and reframes only.
- Every claim comes from the fetched source. Never invent a study or a statistic. "Studies show" is banned.
- Every card is delivered by the narration, one line each.
